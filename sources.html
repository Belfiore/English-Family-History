Sources, and how a claim is worded

Most original images are on Ancestry.com and need an Ancestry login to open. The pictures on this site are clean copies of the entries, with nothing covered. This page is the finding list: the piece and folio, the volume and page, and the register, so the same record can be opened again.

How the evidence is graded

Confirmed. A record states it, and a second independent record agrees, or the original register was read. These sentences do not need a hedge.

Probable. The records point the same way, but a certificate, or a second reading of a register, would still help. On these pages that is the meaning of probably, about and likely.

Family tradition. Remembered in the family, and not found in a record. It is labelled as tradition. Handwriting on the backs of the family's photographs, the 1950 album captions and the medallion inscription are treated as family evidence. They pointed the searches that found the passenger lists and the 1936 notice.

Leads, low confidence guesses, and links the records rule out are not stated as facts. The London Gazette of 23 April 1890 rules out Edward Dixon as a son of the Earls of Orkney. A published account on fitzmaurice-family.com places John FitzMaurice in a Carlow branch. Its London details, Eliza Taylor, the three sons, and William's date of birth, now agree with the registers. His birthplace and his parents are still not on a record, so that origin is likely but not proved, and it is not drawn on the tree. Patricia and Ronald Andrews have died, so their years are given. Everyone else who may still be living is named with their relationship only. Historic addresses of people who have died are shown in full.
