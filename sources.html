Sources, and how a claim is worded

The interactive tree keeps a source note on each fact. This page is the finding list: the piece and folio, the volume and page, and the register, so the same record can be opened again. Ancestry collection numbers are the ones used when the image was read.

How the evidence is graded

Confirmed. A record states it, and a second independent record agrees, or the original register was read. These sentences do not need a hedge.

Probable. The records point the same way, but a certificate, or a second reading of a register, would still help. On these pages that is the meaning of probably, about and likely.

Family tradition. Remembered in the family, and not found in a record. It is labelled as tradition. The clerical collar in a family photograph is in this class: the records show a civil engineer.

Leads, low confidence guesses, and links the records rule out are not stated as facts. That includes a descent from the Earls of Orkney, which the London Gazette of 23 April 1890 rules out. People who may still be living are named with their relationship only.
